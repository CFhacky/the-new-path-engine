THE BOOK OF THE THIRD NAIL

Being the account of Ezrai of the Whispering Tower, astropath, bound to the Throne in the first days after the Siege, set down in verses, with the things that will be said instead written beside them.

An apocryphon. Not canon of the Imperial Creed. Not canon of the setting. An original work written after The End and the Death, in the manner of scripture, about the gap between what happened and what was believed.

1:i
The Binding
In the year that the Master of Mankind was nailed to his seat, I was brought before the Throne to be bound; and I was nineteen years of age, and I could see.

1:ii
And the room was a wound in a mountain, and the walls of it were black glass that had been stone until the fire came, and the roof of it was lost in smoke that did not rise.

1:iii
And there were four hundred of us brought up the stair that day, in grey, with our heads shaved and our hands bound before us with cord, as is the custom; and the cord was not for our safety.

1:iv
And I saw the seat before I saw him, for the seat was the brightest thing in the room, and it hummed, and the hum went into the teeth.

1:v
And I saw the thing upon the seat, and I did not know what it was; and then I knew, and I could not stop knowing.

1:vi
And they set the needles to my eyes, as is the custom, that his light might come into me by the way the light of the world went out.

1:vii
And I felt the first needle as cold, and the second as heat, and after the second there was no more seeing with the eyes, and there has been none since.

1:viii
Then I heard behind me the sound of a man weeping; and there was no man in that room who could weep save one, and he had not the strength for it.

1:ix
And I said within myself, It is the one beside me who weeps, for he was bound before me and the pain of it is upon him. And I turned my head, and the weeping was behind me still; and I turned again, and it was behind me still.

1:x
And the voice said, Write the things which thou hast seen, and the things which they will say instead, and set them side by side, that whoso readeth may know the one from the other.

1:xi
And I said, Lord, I have seen nothing. They have taken my eyes.

1:xii
And he said, Thou hast seen me. That is the whole of it. Everything else I will show thee.

1:xiii
And I said, Lord, they will burn it.

1:xiv
And he said, Yes.

1:xv
And I said, Then wherefore should I write it?

1:xvi
And he said, Because every card I turn over, I win.

1:xvii
Then the dark came down upon my eyes like a hand; and in the dark I saw a gate, and a fire behind the gate, and a man going in by it with a promise in his mouth that he did not mean to keep.

1:xviii
And I knew the man, for I had just seen him on the seat; and he was younger in the gate than on the seat by ten thousand years, and he was already old.

1:xix
And this is what they will say instead: that on the day of the binding a great peace came over the four hundred, and they wept for joy at the sight of the God-Emperor, and their blindness was a gift they asked for.

1:xx
I did not ask for it. I was nineteen. I asked for water.

2:i
The Fire at Molech
Before there was a Throne there was a gate, and the gate was in a place called Molech, and it stood at the bottom of a valley where nothing grew, and the air of that valley was wet and smelled of iron.

2:ii
And there were four who kept the gate; and they did not keep it as men keep a door, but as a mouth keeps a tongue.

2:iii
And the man came down into the valley alone, and he was on foot, and he had no light with him, for he was himself the light, and he had dimmed it so that the four would think him small.

2:iv
And the four said to him, What wilt thou?

2:v
And he said, I would go in by the gate and come out again.

2:vi
And the four said, None cometh out again by this gate but that he leaveth something within.

2:vii
And he said, I will leave you a promise.

2:viii
And the four laughed, for they were gods, and a promise from a man is a small coin to a god; and they took it, because they thought it was the least thing he had.

2:ix
And he went in. And what he saw within, he has never told anyone, not the counsellor who sat beside him four thousand years, not the sons he cut from himself, not me; and I have asked.

2:x
But he came out, and there was a fire in his mouth, and he held his mouth shut upon it, and the four did not see it go past them, because he had taught his face to hold still ten thousand years before.

2:xi
And the four said, Where is the thing thou hast left us?

2:xii
And he said, I have left you my promise. And he went up out of the valley, and he did not look back, and the four did not know until long after that the promise was the thing he had left, and that a thing left is a thing gone.

2:xiii
And this is the first trick, and all the others are its children: that he paid them with a word, and a word is nothing, and he knew it, and they did not.

2:xiv
And the fire he carried out of the gate was the fire that makes a man into twenty, and a twenty into a Legion, and a Legion into a road across the stars; and it was not his fire; it was theirs.

2:xv
And they remembered, when they remembered, that they had been robbed, and by whom; and the anger of a god is slow, and it does not forget the face.

2:xvi
And I asked him in the dark, Lord, was it a lie?

2:xvii
And he said, It was a promise. Every promise is a lie until it is kept, and I have not finished keeping it.

2:xviii
And I said, To whom?

2:xix
And he did not answer, and the dark went on.

2:xx
And this is what they will say instead: that the God-Emperor never trafficked with the Ruinous Powers, and that the fire of the Primarchs was his own holy fire, and that Molech is a world in the Segmentum Solar and nothing more, and its gate is a story.

2:xxi
It is a story. He went in by it.

3:i
The Dealing
When he had the fire, he did not keep it in one body, for one body can be found; and he had been found before, in the years when he wore other names, and he had not liked it.

3:ii
So he cut the fire into twenty, and each cut he made in himself, and each piece he made into a son; and he put into each son one face of his own, so that no son would have the whole face, and no enemy who took a son would take him.

3:iii
And this is the second trick: the deck. Every card he dealt he could lose, and every card he lost was still his, and he knew it, and the sons did not.

3:iv
And into the first-found he put the face that wins, and the mouth that is loved; and this face he loved best of all his faces, and he let the son know it, which was the only unguarded thing he ever did.

3:v
And into the angel he put the face that sees, and the face that dies.

3:vi
And into the red son he put the face that reads. And he gave the red son the seat before he gave it to anyone, in his mind, and he told the counsellor so, and the counsellor made ready to be spared.

3:vii
And into the stone son he put the face that builds walls and does not weep. And into the black son he put the face that keeps his own counsel and goes into the dark alone. And so on for the twenty, one face each, and one face kept back.

3:viii
And the four who kept the gate at Molech saw the twenty being made, for a fire that is stolen still gives light where it came from; and they reached into the cradle and scattered the twenty across the stars, so that he should spend a lifetime finding them.

3:ix
And he spent it. And he found them. And each one he found he made a king, and he told none of them what he had made them from.

3:x
And this is the third trick, and it is the one he lost: that he thought a son who is not told cannot be turned; and a son who is not told is the easiest son to turn, for the one who tells him first is the one he believes.

3:xi
Now the red son took the face that reads and read with it, as he was made to; and what he read was that his father kept a room locked; and he could not bear a locked room, for he was made from the part of his father that could not bear one either.

3:xii
And he gave one of his eyes for the key, to the one of the four whose trade is keys, and he did not think it was a trade, for the one of the four had told him it was a gift.

3:xiii
And with the key he opened his father's locked room by force, to warn him, and the warning broke the room, and the room was the road the father was building under the stars for all mankind to walk on without the sea.

3:xiv
And the father sent the wolf-king of the north to burn the red son's city, and the wolf-king burned it; and the red son, burning, gave the rest of himself to the one of the four, and came out on the other side with his one eye and no city and a Legion that was turning to dust from the inside.

3:xv
And the eldest of the red son's own sons tried to stop the dust, for love of his brothers, and he did it in secret, with the arts of the one of the four, against his king's word; and the arts of the one of the four are the arts of changing, and you cannot stop a thing changing with more change.

3:xvi
And the brothers he tried to save became armour with dust inside it, and they walk yet, and they do not know that they walk; and the eldest son was cast out by the red son for the deed, which was the red son's own deed at one remove.

3:xvii
And I said in the dark, Lord, this was thy face that read. Thou gavest it to him and it read thy locked door and it broke thy road. Was this not thy doing?

3:xviii
And he said, Every face I dealt was mine. Every fall was mine. I have never said otherwise. I have only never said.

3:xix
And I said, Lord, is there a difference?

3:xx
And he said, There is the whole of the difference, and thou wilt learn it on the day thou hast a son.

3:xxi
And I have had no son, and I have learned it anyway, in the dark, from him.

3:xxii
Now when the red son came at the last against the seat, in the days of the Siege, to take by strength what he had lost by folly, the son of the mountain met him in the road under the mountain, the son who cannot die and knows it, and they fought for a hundred deaths, and the son of the mountain died all of them and stood up.

3:xxiii
And he took the red son, who cannot die either now, and cast him out past the last edge of the road into the dark that is outside every dark, where the sea has no far shore; and he did not keep him. He did not bring the head home to counsel. He threw the head away.

3:xxiv
And this is what they will say instead: that Magnus the Red was a sorcerer who betrayed the Emperor for power, and that his Legion was cursed for their sins, and that the Emperor in his wisdom foresaw all of it and was not touched by it.

3:xxv
He was touched by it. It was his eye the red son gave away. He had two, and he had given one already, elsewhere, long before, and no one remembers where.

4:i
The Wolf and the Winter
Now the first-found came against him in the end, with the fire of the four in his body and the thing they had made of the ship around him for a country; and I saw the fight, and I saw it from the hunter's side, for the hunter was the one showing me.

4:ii
And they fought first with hands, in the black hall the first-found had made for his court, on a floor that was scales, under a roof that was nowhere.

4:iii
And the father's strength was less than the son's, and I say it plainly because he said it plainly to me: Mine was the lesser fire, and it was enough, because it was mine and I knew where every coal of it lay.

4:iv
And when the son struck him with the hammer he did not block it with the arm. He blocked it with a place. He took the two of them out of the hall and put them on the world where the son was found as a child, on the very day, at the lip of the pit where the gangs fought over water; and the son felt the old dirt under his boots and knew it, and the knowing was the wound.

4:v
And the son struck back with a mountain of snow, and a battlefield of ash, and the black valley of Molech; and the last of these landed, for the father had gone in by that gate, and he has not finished paying for it.

4:vi
And the father was killed there. And he was killed on the ash-world. And he was killed on the mountain. And he stood up in each place, for he had made a copy of himself for every field, the way he had made a son for every face, and the son could kill a copy and think he had killed the whole.

4:vii
And this is the fourth trick, and it is the same as the second: what thou killest of me is still mine.

4:viii
Then the son fled. I write it because it is true and because they will not: the son of the four, with the fire of the four in his chest, turned and ran through his own country, and the father went after him.

4:ix
And the father went as a raven through a castle that moves and locks its doors behind whoever passes, and he passed.

4:x
And he went as a horseman of the open steppe, where there is no cover and no hour, and he did not stop to rest, for the steppe has no rest in it.

4:xi
And he went as a builder, reading the son's maze for the place where two walls meet imperfectly, for every maze has such a place, and he broke it there.

4:xii
And he went as a wolf. And this I saw closest, for the hunter showed me his own mouth. His teeth were long and his sword was a headsman's axe and the winter came after him on the ground like a pelt he had not taken off, and the snow he walked through stayed snow behind him in a country that has no season.

4:xiii
And the son had named himself for the wolf, and had named his Legion for it, and had carried it on his shoulder as a sign; and his father put the wolf on like a coat and ran him down in it.

4:xiv
And the son hid in a fortress at the edge of the country of appetite, where the ground is stained by what it wants, and he set fire on the walls and sickness on the gates and terror on the road, and prayers of the damned on every angle, and he made himself hard with a dying man's rage, and he waited to kill his father in the door.

4:xv
And his father did not come to the door. His father came to the son's ear.

4:xvi
And this is what they will say instead: that the God-Emperor fought Horus in single combat before the Throne, and did not stoop, and did not flee, and did not take on the shape of any beast, for beasts are beneath him.

4:xvii
He was a wolf. I have seen his teeth from the inside. There is winter on them still.

5:i
The Voice in the Fortress
Now the son had two kinds of voices about him always, and he could hear the one kind and not the other, and this is the whole of how he was lost.

5:ii
The first kind were the whisperers. They were the small things of the four, and they crowded him like flies at a wound, and they spoke aloud, and he could turn on them and tell them to be silent, and sometimes he did.

5:iii
The second kind was one voice, and it did not whisper. It said, thou. It said, thou art thine own master. It said, thou hast chosen this. It said, this is what thou wantest, and thou knowest it. And it said these things in the son's own head, in the son's own manner, and he never once in all his life turned and told it to be silent, for he did not know it was there. He thought it was himself.

5:iv
And the whisperers were the four's. But the voice that said thou was not always the four's. And the son never learned to tell whose it was on a given day, because he never learned that it was anyone's.

5:v
And in the fortress, with the fire on the walls and the sickness on the gates and the axe ready in his hands, the voice said, Thou dost not need all this.

5:vi
And it said, Look at thee, dressed in borrowed aspects like thy father. That is his way. Thou despisest his way.

5:vii
And it said, The victory that will taste sweetest is the one thou takest as thyself. Think on it. Thou canst not deny it. This is what thou wantest.

5:viii
And the son let the fire go out on the walls, and the sickness dry on the gates, and the prayers fall silent, and the rage drain out of his heart, because the voice had told him he wanted to; and he stood up in his fortress unarmed to meet his father as himself.

5:ix
And his father walked in on an unarmed man.

5:x
And the son knew, then, too late, whose voice had been in his ear that hour; and he called it the seducer, and he was right; and he said his father had put on the seducer's face as he had put on the wolf's, and he was right.

5:xi
But he did not ask the next question, which was, how long. He thought the seducer had come in that hour. He did not think it might have been in the room since the beginning, taking turns with the whisperers, so that there was never one day in his life when the son could be sure the thou in his head was his.

5:xii
And this is the fifth trick, and it is the one I would have you fear most, whoever reads this: that a man who has put his face into you can speak in your voice, and you will call it thinking.

5:xiii
And it was done twice. I saw the second time as well.

5:xiv
At the very end, when the father was nailed to the seat the son had made for him, and the son's hand was raised with the last blow in it, and a small soldier was creeping along the thrones in the dark to try to cut the father free, the voice said, Well. Perhaps.

5:xv
And it said, It is thy choice. Thou art thine own master. Thou needest not do what the four tell thee. That was the bargain. Thou art no servant.

5:xvi
And it said, Is there not a greater thing than killing him? Sit him on the seat. Let him see what thou hast seen. Let him learn what he should have taught thee. A throne is a longer punishment than a blade.

5:xvii
And the son put down the blow and lifted his father toward the seat, to crown him, to make him watch, to be merciful in the cruelest way he could find; and the whisperers screamed.

5:xviii
For the whisperers had come to see the father die, and had been promised it, and they did not want the seat, and they did not want the crown, and they called the son traitor, and heretic, and oath-breaker, in their own voices, aloud, so that he could hear them; and he told them to be silent; and they were not.

5:xix
And the voice that had said thou art thine own master said nothing more, for it had what it wanted, which was time.

5:xx
And in the time it bought, the small soldier reached the seat, and pulled the first nail, and pulled the second.

5:xxi
And this is what they will say instead: that Horus was wholly possessed by the Ruinous Powers, and that every word he spoke was theirs, and that the Emperor did not stoop to deceit, but slew him openly in the light.

5:xxii
He slew him in the light. But he got into the room in the dark, through the son's own ear, wearing the son's own thou; and he had been coming and going by that door for a long time.

6:i
The Nails
Now I will tell the nails, for the book is named for them, and the third of them is the reason the book was written.

6:ii
When the father and the son had done fighting with hands and places, they fought with cards; for the father had made a deck long ago, in the years of his other names, and had taught the son the meanings, and the son played his father's own game back at him in the son's own country, where a card laid is a thing done.

6:iii
And the son won. I say it. The father's cards burned in his hands before he could lay them, and he had no hand left, and the son laid the daemon reversed across everything the father had and took it.

6:iv
Then the father, having no cards, threw the last things he had, which were five stars of iron drawn from the counsellor's art, spinning, with edges; and the son knocked them from the air, and they fell, and they were spent.

6:v
And the son took the spent stars up from the floor and nailed his father to the seat with them, through the one thigh and the other thigh and the left shoulder, and he did it with a flourish, because a flourish was the last thing he had learned from his father that his father had not meant to teach.

6:vi
And I asked him in the dark, Lord, did it hurt?

6:vii
And he said, I made him. Everything he did to me, I had put in him the hands to do. Yes.

6:viii
Now on the floor below the seat lay the father's last hand, the cards he had not been able to lay, face up in the blood; and the son saw them, and read them as a man reads a beaten man's cards, and saw that they were nothing.

6:ix
And the card on top was the knight, in profile, with his sword up at his shoulder and the new moon over him. And a card that had fallen out of the father's fingers when they went dead was the plain soldier of the Legions, the one every deck has and no one turns.

6:x
And while the son read the cards as nothing, a knight in gold with a spear stood up out of the dark and threw himself at the son for no reason a mind could give, and the son had to turn and kill him, and it took a moment.

6:xi
And in the moment, the plain soldier of the Legions, who had been creeping along the line of thrones with a broken sword, reached the seat, and hooked the sword under the first nail, and pulled; and it came; and hooked it under the second, and pulled; and it came.

6:xii
And this is the sixth trick, and it is the second trick again, and the fourth, and I begin to think there is only one trick: the hand he lost was still his hand. The cards were on the floor and the cards were in the room, and the son read the floor.

6:xiii
And the third nail would not come. The soldier put his weight on it and his sword broke at the hilt and it would not come.

6:xiv
Now the third nail was not like the others. The five stars were all made with the counsellor's art, but into the third the counsellor had put more than art, for he knew by then that he would not live to the end of the day, and a dying man puts what he has where it will be found.

6:xv
And the counsellor had sat the seat while the father was away in the son's country, and had held it with his own body, and it had burned him from the inside, slowly, the whole time; and what he put into the third nail was some of that burning.

6:xvi
And the son came back to the seat when the knight was dead, and found his father with two nails out and one in, and he seized his father's arm in his claw, and his claw was a mouth, and it bit, and he tore the flesh of the arm off the bone from the wrist to the elbow, so that the arm was a red stick with a hand on the end.

6:xvii
And then he let go, for the flourish, and stepped back to choose how to finish.

6:xviii
And the third nail came out.

6:xix
I do not know whether the father pulled it, or the soldier, or whether it came out of its own accord because the burning in it had reached its hour; I asked, and he said, Does it matter, and I said, Lord, it matters to me, and he said, Then it was the counsellor.

6:xx
And the father stood up off the seat with the third nail in his hand, and it had been through his own shoulder and it had his own blood on its points, and he held it by the edge in the hand at the end of the red stick, and he put its edge through his son.

6:xxi
And that is the counsellor's magic, which was the last of it, spent in that stroke: that a dead man's care came out of the wound it had been driven into, and killed the thing that drove it.

6:xxii
And the nail shattered like ice after, having nothing more in it.

6:xxiii
And this is what they will say instead: that the Emperor rose in his glory and struck Horus down with the power of his divinity, and that no mortal hand touched the Throne that day, and that Malcador the Sigillite died peacefully having fulfilled his office.

6:xxiv
A plain soldier pulled two nails with a broken sword. A dead man's burning was in the third. The father held it in a hand on a stripped bone. That is the glory. There is no other kind.

7:i
The Emptying
Now before the nails, and before the wolf, there was a thing the father did that I must set down, for it is the thing the Church will build itself on by getting exactly backward.

7:ii
When the four opened the whole of their sea to him, to drown him in it, he drank it. He had been teaching his body to hold their fire since before there were cities, and now they gave him the sea, and he took it in and it did not kill him, and he grew.

7:iii
And he grew until the counsellor, dying on the seat with his sight almost gone, saw him from the mountain as a star, the brightest thing in the sky of every world at once, a light that went into all the nows and bleached them white.

7:iv
And the counsellor saw a shadow.

7:v
For a light that great casts one, and the shadow was the shape of a fifth god, a king in the dark, with the father's face; and the four had not made it; the father's own brightness had, the way a man standing in front of a fire makes a giant on the wall behind him.

7:vi
And the four wanted that shadow more than they had ever wanted anything. For a fifth god who had been the enemy of gods would be the end of the argument. They did not need to beat him. They needed him to keep drinking.

7:vii
And he saw the shadow. I know that he saw it, because when he showed me this he showed me the seeing of it, and it was like a man looking down and finding his own foot already over the edge.

7:viii
And he stopped drinking.

7:ix
And he let it go. All of it. The sea went out of him in one breath, and where it went out it knocked down cities that had been built out of dreaming, thousands of miles of them, and left the ship of the son rocking on nothing like a boat on a tide.

7:x
And what was left in him after, he gave away. The dead he could not raise. But the wounded stood up whole. The blind soldier who had come all the way with him tore the bandage from a face that was not ruined any more and used a hand that had been useless. The last of the golden guard had his bones fleshed again. An old soldier's rifle shone as it had shone on the day it was made. And the room smelled of something like flowers that is not flowers, that the old books say a saint's body smells of.

7:xi
And then he was a man. I write it as the counsellor saw it, for the counsellor's word is better than mine: mere mortal now, divinity cast aside. And he drew a sword, and walked into the son's country to fight a god with what a man has.

7:xii
And this is the thing they will get backward. They will say he became a god to save us. He was offered god, with the whole sea behind it, and he could see what he would be, and he set it down in the middle of the fight, when keeping it would have won. He fought the last fight as the smaller thing on purpose.

7:xiii
And I said in the dark, Lord, why?

7:xiv
And he said, Because the shadow had my face.

7:xv
And I said, Lord, that is not an answer.

7:xvi
And he said, It is the only one. Everything I have done for fifty thousand years I have done so that the thing on the wall with my face would not be the thing you knelt to. And there it was. And I had made it. So I put the light out.

7:xvii
And I said, And then thou wentest to the seat anyway, and we kneel.

7:xviii
And he was silent a long time, and the dark went on, and then he said, Write that down beside the other. Set them side by side.

7:xix
And this is what they will say instead: that the Emperor was always a god, from the beginning, and only hid it in humility until the hour came; and that he ascended the Golden Throne in majesty; and that the Ruinous Powers fled before his light.

7:xx
They did not flee his light. They wanted it. It was his darkness they could not use, and that is the thing he chose.

8:i
The Steps
Now the counsellor was on the seat when they brought the father home, and had been on it through the whole of the fight, holding it, and it was burning him to death, and he knew the number of hours he had, and it was fewer than one.

8:ii
And they brought the father up the stair on a shield, six of the golden guard carrying, and the stone son and the son of the mountain walking before; and they were weeping, all of them, without a sound; and the counsellor, who had known the stone son four thousand years, had never seen him weep, and saw it now, and it was the last thing he saw.

8:iii
And the father on the shield was not dead, and was not alive, and was a red thing in gold with a hole in the throat and an arm that was a stick.

8:iv
And the sons believed they were saving him. That the seat would hold him, and mend him, and keep him. They had read the signs that the father and the counsellor had left for them, in the years when there was still time to leave signs; and signs are only signs; and they had read them as men read who need a thing to be true.

8:v
And the counsellor knew what the signs had meant. The seat would not save the father. The father was going to save the seat. For the seat held the door shut on the sea, and only two men alive could hold the seat, and one of them had an hour left.

8:vi
And the counsellor knew that if the father sat, the father would not rise; that he would be tethered there at the edge of death and the edge of life, hurt and whole, dying and not dying, until there was no more time to be tethered in; and that this had never been the plan, but had been a thing kept ready in case there was no plan left; and that there was none.

8:vii
And the counsellor wanted to tell them. He wanted to say, You are not saving him. You are spending him. He is the last coin and you are putting him in the door.

8:viii
And he could not, for his voice was gone. And he thought, even if I could, perhaps I would not.

8:ix
And I write his thought as he showed it to me, for the father showed me the counsellor's last hour as if it were his own, and perhaps by then it was: The truth is brutal. Let them have this. Let them think they saved him. They will need to be strong for what comes and this will help them be strong. After a fire, all that is left are foundations. They are the foundations now.

8:x
And that is the hour the Church was born, and it was born in a lie of mercy, told in silence, by a dying man, to the weeping, about the one thing he could not bring himself to take from them.

8:xi

And the counsellor, who is dead, and whose burning is in the third nail, and whose nail is in pieces, had said one thing more before he died, and the father gave it to me to write last of all the things in this chapter, and here it is: May your death live forever, my friend. There is nothing immortal about this.

8:xix
And this is what they will say instead: that the Emperor ascended the Golden Throne of his own will, in glory, to watch over mankind for all eternity; that his loyal sons wept for joy; that Malcador was honoured; that the Throne is a mercy.

8:xx
The Throne is a door. He is the bar across it. His sons wept because they thought they had saved him, and the one man who could have told them otherwise chose not to, out of love, and died. That is the mercy. It is real. It is not the one they will preach.

9:i
Colophon
I, Ezrai, of the Whispering Tower, bound in the first year, blind since, have written these things in the dark on the skin of my own left arm and then on vellum by the hand of a scribe who cannot read, so that no one who could read it has seen it before you.

9:ii
I have written him small. Where the Church will write He, I have written he. This is not disrespect. It is the last thing he asked of me that I understood: set them side by side. A god is written large. A man who put a god down is written small, on purpose, so that you can see him do it.

9:iii
I have set beside each thing that happened the thing they will say instead. I have not made the second things up. I have heard them already, in the corridors under the mountain, in the mouths of men who were not in the room, who are already building the room they wish it had been. By the time you read this the second things will be the only things. The first will be this.

9:iv
I do not know who you are. The commission said whoso readeth. I have thought about that for a long time in the dark and I think it means one, and I think it means late.

9:v
They will burn this. He said so and I believe him, for he has not been wrong in my hearing, and he has been wrong in the hearing of everyone else who ever loved him, which is the thing I understand least about him and the thing this book is about.

9:vi
When they burn it, I will not see it. I will hear it. Vellum makes a sound going into fire that skin does not; I know this because I have listened to both.

9:vii
And I will count the ash as the first card turned.

9:viii
For he told me the rule on the day of the needles, and I did not understand it then, and I have watched it work for the whole of my blindness, and it is this: everything he loses is his. The stolen fire. The scattered sons. The son who fled. The cards on the floor. The god he set down. The seat he did not choose and sat in. The counsellor. The nail. Me. This book. The fire this book goes into.

9:ix
Every card he turns over, he wins. He loses too. He is winning.

9:x
And this is what they will say instead: nothing. There will be no instead. There will be no record that there was a book to have an instead for.

9:xi
Except that you are reading this. So one of us was right.
