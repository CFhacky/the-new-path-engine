## 1. What Kozakura is for

**One continent, two registers.**

| | **Shou Lung / Banang** (the strategic theater) | **Kozakura (and Wa)** (the mythic theater) |
|---|---|---|
| Governing documents | Kara-Tur Strategic Survey and simulations (S) | **This primer** |

| Magic | Counted, gated and costed; "no free rainfall" (S) | **Everywhere and local.** Every spring has an owner. |

**The line between the two is the sea.** The survey's rule that Kozakura and Red Gate hooks are not imported into the Banang theater without evidence of reach (S, Extraordinary Force Register) stands. This primer is the other half of that rule: **inside Kozakura, the myths are the evidence.** Crossing the strait is crossing registers. Shou's bureaucratic heaven still exists over Kozakura, but it is distant and slow, and the local kami answer first.

**Canon already built in this register (S).** This primer generalizes what they already do; it does not replace them.

## 2. The Law: Myth Pressure

> *In Kozakura the old stories are not finished. They are looking for people to play their parts.*

### 2.1 When a rhyme starts (P)
The GM declares a **rhyme** with a known myth when a character's situation matches it on **two or more** of four axes:

| Axis | Matches when… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Act** | They do what a figure in the myth did | Accept shelter from the poor after the rich refused (Somin Shōrai) |
| **Place** | They stand where it happened, or somewhere of the same kind | A river-mouth, a demon-gate ridge, a slope down into the dark |
| **Token** | They carry its object, or its number | A comb; a peach; *eight* of anything; a sword taken from a serpent |
| **Role** | Someone already treats them as the figure | Kusanagi kneeling to Arik as Susanoo |

Three axes give a **strong rhyme** (a bigger clock, faster ticks). Four is **casting**: the myth has chosen its actor.

### 2.2 The clock
- **Size by the myth's weight:** 4 segments for a folk tale (Kobutori, the Crane Wife), 6 for a hero legend (Momotarō, Raikō and Shuten-dōji), 8 for a divine myth (Susanoo, Izanagi's flight).
- **It ticks** when the character takes another action that rhymes, at each thin hour spent in a thin place (§5), and when an NPC treats them as the figure.
- **Each tick brings a nudge.** The world leans the story forward. Roll 1d6, or choose:

| d6 | Nudge |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Weather** takes the myth's side: hail, fog, a wind from the wrong quarter |
| 2 | **Coincidence.** The myth's object turns up: a comb on the road, a peach in the offering bowl. |
| 3 | **An NPC takes their place in the cast.** The old couple, the rival brother or the princess appears, played by a local who has no idea why they're acting that way. |
| 4 | **Omen animals:** crows, deer, foxes, a snake on the threshold |
| 5 | **A dream** of the next beat, seen from inside the figure |
| 6 | **The road bends.** Directions, ferries and closed passes steer toward the beat's location. |

### 2.3 When the clock fills: the Beat
The myth's next beat arrives as a scene. The character either **plays it** or **breaks it**.

- **Play the beat → the myth pays.** The character gains a **Boon** taken from the figure's story, lasting until the next dawn, or **permanently if the myth completes** (all of its beats played).
	- Boons are concrete and myth-shaped, for example:
		- the Kobutori dancer's **favor of the oni** (one free bargain);
		- Momotarō's **three companions** (the next three animals met become allies for a day);
		- Susanoo's **storm at his back** (weather obeys one command).
	- 3.5e: a supernatural ability of the GM's design, CL equal to character level. GURPS: a temporary Advantage worth 10–30 points, permanent only on completion.
- **Break the beat → the myth bites.** The character makes a **Will save (DC 15 + clock size)** / **Will roll at −(clock size ÷ 2)**.
	- **Success:** the clock resets to half and the story waits.
	- **Failure:** the character is **Unfinished**. They take −2 on all reactions with kami and yokai until the beat is played or someone else plays it.
- **Recasting.** If the actor refuses twice, **the myth recasts.** An NPC becomes the figure and plays the beat in their stead, usually worse. Someone else becomes the Susanoo, the Momotarō or the old woman with the rice.
	- **This is the realm's most dangerous rule.** A myth refused by a hero goes looking for a villain.

### 2.4 Reading and steering pressure
- **Onmyōji, kannushi, yamabushi** (and anyone with Knowledge (religion) 10+ ranks / Occultism-14 who has studied Kozakuran stories) can read an active rhyme: DC 20 to name the myth, 25 for the next beat.
- **Deliberate casting** is possible and dangerous. Acting a myth on purpose to borrow its Boon gives +1 tick per scene, and **the myth knows**: the Beat arrives early and in its harshest form.
- **Two myths on one actor** fight. The character takes the *Unfinished* penalty from both until one completes.

### 2.5 Worked examples
- **Arik in Kozakura (P, the arc's core danger).** Storm King + Kusanagi's kneeling + the Tooth-Marked Jewel gives **Role, Token and Act: casting.** The Susanoo clock (8) **starts at 2**, or **at 4 if the Tooth-Marked Jewel's pledge has been received**, because receiving it plays the oath-contest beat. **Its next beat is the Wrecked Hall.** Every scene in Kozakura pushes Arik toward breaking a sun-court's house. If he breaks the beat, the myth recasts, and the likeliest new Susanoo is **Lorne** (The Two Winners clock) or **Kitsuki Masatane**, who has wanted the seat for five years.
- **The Bout at Kashima (Jewel draft, S5).** A Hand priest wrestling Sone Takamichi for a jewel on shrine sand gives **Act, Place and Role** (Takemikazuchi against Takeminakata). The winner gets the land-taker's Boon: the next contested claim before a kami favors him (+4 reaction).
- **Genbō's forge (S, Akaki).** The Kobutori rhyme is already canon: "the oni take a piece of the face and give it back wrong." That is a folk-tale clock (4) the valley has been running for sixty years.

## 3. The kami: every place has an owner

> *Eight million gods, the old phrase goes. Most of them own one thing.*

### 3.1 The kami block (P; use it for every site)
Every kami gets four lines. That is the whole interface.
- **Domain:** what it owns (a spring, a pass, a cedar, a bay, a craft).
- **Appetite:** what it wants offered (rice, sake, salt, a song, a sword's first cut, silence).
- **Rule:** the one thing it will not tolerate (blood on the stones, iron in the water, a lie spoken at the gate, women on the island, peach at the threshold).
- **Gift:** what it gives to those who keep the rule and feed the appetite.

### 3.2 Scale
| Scale | Examples | Reach | When offended |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Household** | Hearth, well, privy, a family's ancestors | One house | Bad luck: −1 on all rolls in the house until amends are made |
| **Site** | A spring, a bridge, an old tree, a crossroads, the Ridge Cedar | One place, up to a mile | The site turns: a ford rises, a bridge drops, a path loops back on itself. **A Myth Pressure tick** on anything that rhymes. |
| **Province** | A mountain (Shirone-dake), a great river, a sea-lane, the island daughter on Okitsu | A valley, a coast, a strait | Weather, plague, failed harvests, sea-curses; priests send for help |
| **Heavenly** | Amaterasu's court, the office's seat, Kashima, the sword-daughters | Everywhere the myth is told | Agents arrive (Sone Takamichi), and lawsuits in Heaven |

### 3.3 Purity and pollution (*kegare*) (P)
This is the realm's background mechanic, and it explains why the island and the purity mountain behave the way they do.
- **Gaining pollution points (PP):**
	- touching a corpse or shedding blood in a sacred place: +1;
	- killing in a shrine's precinct: +3;
	- eating the food of the dead: +3, plus the binding;
	- breaking a kami's Rule: +2.
- **At 3+ PP:**
	- −2 on reactions with kami and shrine folk;
	- **a torii will not let you pass**: you physically falter, and it takes a Will save, DC 15 + PP, to step through;
	- prayers to local kami go unanswered.
- **Clearing it:**
	- **misogi** (washing in moving or sea water, 1 PP per immersion, Fort DC 15 in winter);
	- **harae** (a priest's wand-waving rite: 2 PP, costs an offering);
	- **salt** (a pinch at a threshold keeps 1 PP outside for one night).
- **Canon fit (S):**
	- Okitsu's washing rule is misogi.
	- Shirone-dake burns oni-marked flesh, which is pollution made physical.
	- The Hand, killers in oni faces, **arrive at 3+ PP on day one.**
- GURPS: track PP as a reaction penalty and a −1 per PP to Religious Ritual and Exorcism skills in Kozakura.

## 4. Yokai: an ecology, not an encounter table

> *Every yokai is a rule wearing a body. Learn the rule and you have a chance. Break it and you have a story.*

### 4.1 The yokai block (P; the Codex will use it)
- **Rule:** the condition it lives by. It can't help it.
- **Tell:** how you know it's here before it shows itself.
- **Weakness:** what the rule makes it vulnerable to.
- **Bargain:** what it will trade, and what it takes in return.
- **Stats:** a 3.5e chassis + CR, and GURPS points + key traits.
- **Knowledge check:** DC 15 + CR/2 / Occultism or Hidden Lore (Yokai) to know the Rule. The Weakness takes 5 more.

## 5. Thin hours and thin places

The spirit world is near everywhere in Kozakura. At these hours and places it leaks. (Kara-Tur's Spirit World is canon, S; this is where it touches ground.)

| Thin hour | When | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Ōmagatoki**, "the hour of meeting demons" | Dusk, the half-hour as the light goes | Encounter checks doubled. **Myth Pressure ticks for any rhyme in progress.** |
| **Ushi-mitsu**, the hour of the ox | 2:00–2:30 a.m. | **Curse-rites work** (the straw doll nailed to a shrine cedar, *ushi no toki mairi*). The dead walk at full strength. |
| **Setsubun** | The eve of spring (S: Midwinter ±1, Akaki calendar) | The oni-out rite. **Oni are driven, or loosed if the rite fails** (Red Gate). |
| **Obon** | High summer, three nights | The dead come home. Ancestors visit their households; **the unburied visit anyone.** Fires on the river lead them back. |
| **New Year's Eve** | The turning of the year | 108 temple bells. **Pollution resets for anyone who hears all 108.** |

| Thin place | Why | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Torii and shrine gates** | Thresholds between owned and unowned ground | The pollution check (§3.3) |
| **Bridges** | Between banks, between worlds | Yokai bargains are binding here, and it's where Ubume wait |
| **Crossroads** | Choice | **Recasting happens here (§2.3).** The myth finds its new actor at a crossroads. |
| **Wells** | Down | Wells connect to the dead (Okiku's well; Ono no Takamura went down a well to serve in Enma's court every night). **A well can be a road to Enma.** |
| **Kimon corners (north-east)** | The demon gate | Oni and yokai traffic. Peach and the Red Gate's rite guard them. |
| **Passes and tunnels** | Mountain thresholds | Tengu tolls, kamaitachi winds, nurikabe |

## 6. Tsukumogami: things that grow souls (P)

> *A tool used for a hundred years wakes up. What it becomes depends on how it was used.*

- **The rule:** an object kept in use for **100 years** in Kozakura (or in a Kozakuran owner's hands) may wake at its hundredth New Year. Roll 3d6, waking on 14+. Add +3 if it was loved, −3 if it was discarded.
- **Temperament** follows its treatment:
	- cared for → a helpful household spirit;
	- discarded → a resentful yokai;
	- **used to kill → a hungry one.**
- **The campaign's reading of its own relics (P):**
	- **Kusanagi** is, to any Kozakuran, a **sword that grew a woman**, a tsukumogami. The chroniclers' lie (the Kusanagi page: "the legend's sword is a woman") is the local *truth*. Shrines will treat her as a sacred object first and a person second. **She will hate it, and it will feed her.**
	- **Eternal Dancers** read as **tsukumogami made on purpose, by industry**: holy to some shrines and monstrous to others. Expect a temple to demand Beatrix or Kusanagi be enshrined, and another to demand they be burned.
	- **Yashiori:** a rite-knife from a Kara-Turan house with an unbroken dawn-list. **If it is a hundred years old (P), it is due to wake**, and the bitten grip suggests it has started.
	- **The Hand's oni masks** are new wood, decades from waking. Unless they're left on Okitsu, where offerings have lain for a thousand years.
- GURPS: an awakened object is a Spirit (Tsukumogami) Ally or Enemy, built per *Spirits*/*Fantasy* guidance, SR.

## 7. How Kozakura reads outsiders (P)

Kozakurans meet a stranger through two questions: **is it a person, or is it a yokai or kami?** And then: **whose rule does it keep?** Westerners get sorted fast, and usually wrongly.

| Outsider | Default reading | Reaction | The consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Drow** (Arik, Lirien) | Dark skin and white hair: **a jorōgumo, an oni, or a kami. Never a man.** | −4 among commoners. Shrines either bar the gate or prostrate themselves (roll 3d6: 3–9 bar, 10–18 prostrate). | Arik cannot be anonymous here. **Every drow walking Kozakura is a myth rhyme waiting to be declared.** |
| **Elves** (Nym, Ivrael) | Tall, long-lived, fine-boned: **mountain-folk, tengu-kin**, or a kami's messenger | Wary respect. Yamabushi test them. | Tengu notice. **A tengu may offer to teach, or to humble.** |
| **Half-elves, humans** | Foreigners. Barbarians from the west. | Neutral to −2 | The baseline |
| **Dwarves** (Durgan) | Short, bearded smiths: **kin to the forge-kami**, or to the earth-spider clans | Smiths' guilds take interest | Durgan's hot blood melting frost reads as **a fire-kami in a man.** |
| **Half-orcs, orcs** | **Oni-blooded** | −4. Bean-throwing. | The Setsubun crowd will drive them out of town on festival night |
| **Constructs** (Kusanagi, Dancers) | **Tsukumogami** (§6) | Shrines claim them | Enshrinement disputes |
| **Masked killers** (the Hand) | **Oni wearing their own faces** | −4, and the peach-born oni-hunter comes (Jewel §6 / peach notes) | The masks are a declaration the whole realm reads |
| **Firearms** (Threadbaum) | **Thunder in a tube:** a thunder god's weapon, or an oni's | Awe or horror | A gunshot in a shrine precinct is +3 PP and a Myth Pressure tick toward **Raijin**, the thunder god |

## 8. The Night Parade (Hyakki Yagyō) (P)

> *On certain nights the hundred demons walk the streets in procession. You stay inside, or you see it.*

- **When:** fixed nights in the Kozakuran calendar, called *night-parade days* (six a year in the old almanacs). The onmyōji publish them. **Roll 1d6 per week of travel; on a 6, a night-parade day falls this week** and the almanac knew.
- **What:** a procession half a mile long. Oni with lanterns, tsukumogami umbrellas and sandals, kappa, a nue overhead, the occasional great oni-lord carried in a palanquin. It is **CR 18+ as a body**, and the point is never to fight it.
- **Seeing it:** whoever sees the Parade must save (Will DC 22) or **be seen back.** Being seen means taken, eaten, or recruited into the procession as a tsukumogami (P).
- **Protections (S, from folklore):**
	- the **Sonshō Darani**, a sutra charm sewn into a collar, saved one courtier by bursting into flames that the demons fled;
	- staying inside with the shutters closed;
	- having **already been cast** in a myth: the Parade will not take an actor before his beat.
- **Use:** a calendar hazard on the pilgrimage road; a set-piece; and a recruitment ground. **The Parade is where a myth goes to find a new actor when it has been refused too often.**

## 9. The human layer

**Period frame (P):** warring states. A weak court, feuding warlords, and temples and shrine-houses that field armed monks and hold land. Canon already has Kozakuran temples with substantial political interests (S, Eastern Interoperability §7) and a local lord at Sakaibara Hold with 40 ashigaru (S, Akaki). **No named emperor, shogun or 1498 ruler is imported from the published baseline** (S, survey date rule). Names are invented at need, under the naming-collision protocol.

| Who | What they do in a Kozakuran story |
|---|---|
| **Onmyōji** (court diviners) | Read rhymes and thin hours, publish the almanac, and command **shikigami** (paper familiars). **Directional taboos:** when a direction is closed, you detour (*katatagae*), and the road bends (Myth Pressure nudge 6). This is the realm's own magic tradition. |
| **Yamabushi** (mountain ascetics) | Walk the purity mountains, deal with tengu, and blow conch horns. Shirone-dake's walled-in holy man is one (S). |
| **Sohei** (warrior monks) | Temple armies. The Yakumo-ha's twenty (Jewel draft). |
| **Kannushi and miko** | Shrine priests and shrine maidens. Kagura dances, harae, misogi. Ichiki Shōun (Jewel draft). |
| **Ronin** | Masterless swords. **The natural adventuring class**, and the realm's mercenaries for western money. |
| **Ashigaru and lords** | Spear levies and the men who own them. Sakaibara Hold (S). |

## 10. Adventure shapes this realm supports

1. **The road.** A pilgrimage highway of **stations**, each one a town with its kami, its yokai and its story. This is the realm's spine and it is fully modular. *Next document.*
2. **The rule-hunt.** A yokai plagues a valley. The job is to **learn its Rule** (investigation, GURPS-side), then use it, bargain, or break it at a cost.
3. **The shrine contract.** A kami's Rule was broken, and someone has to make it right before the province turns.
4. **The mountain apprenticeship.** A tengu offers to teach one technique for one humiliation, which fits the Order of the Storm's Edge's sword-lineage culture.
5. **The myth arc.** A Myth Pressure clock as a campaign spine: someone is being cast, and the arc is whether they play, break or redirect the story. **The Tooth-Marked Jewel is the first.**
6. **The oni island.** The Momotarō pattern: a raid on an oni fortress with three strange allies.

## 11. Guardrails

- **The register line holds.** Kozakuran myth logic does not leak into Banang's simulations without a carried object, an agent or a route (S, survey).
- **Nothing played.** This primer creates mechanics and readings, not events.
- **Strange and dangerous, never cute.** Kitsune lie, kappa drown horses, the Parade eats people. Humour exists (tanuki), and it is the humour of a con that might get you killed.
- **Kami and yokai are not alignment-coded.** They keep rules, and the rules are older than good and evil.
- **Source discipline.** Every yokai and myth used is real folklore, adapted. When a Kara-Tur published source contradicts, the survey's status labels decide.

## 13. The Pale Name in Kozakuran (APPROVED, Chad 7 Oct)

The full entry is in *The Tooth-Marked Jewel* §11.1. In short:

- **The root.** The Nihon Shoki spelling **素戔嗚尊** opens on **素**, "undyed, bare, pale." The Pale Name is the first syllable of the office.
- **Before the claim:**
	- **Shirana-no-Kami** (白名神), "the White-Name God / the Unknown." This is the scholars' name.
	- **Marebito**, the stranger-god, as the villages say it.
	- **Araburu kami**, the Sun court's accusation.
	- Unranked (***mui***).
- **After the claim:** **Takehaya Susanoo-no-Mikoto, Shō Ichii** (Senior First Rank). The plague cults call him **Gozu Tennō**.

## 14. The Calamity: the Summer Flies (P; Chad 7 Oct: "a Ragnarok of a kind")

> *When the Sun hid in the Rock-Cave, the voices of the myriad gods swarmed like summer flies, and every kind of calamity arose.* (Kojiki, the Iwato-gakure)

Kozakura's end-of-the-world story is already written, and the mirror is what ended it. **The Calamity is the cave myth running again:** the Sun is withdrawing, the high order loosens, and the lesser powers fight over the gap. **This is the environment the Tooth-Marked Jewel mission finds** (Uktar–Nightal 1498, Jörmun clock). Nothing in this section has happened in play.

### 14.1 Why the Sun is withdrawing (deliberately ambiguous)
In the myth, she hid because of Susanoo's offenses. In 1498:
- the Susanoo seat is waking (the Aspect wrestling the office, the vision of 12 Uktar);
- the bitten bead is moving;
- the Yashiori are feeding.

**Nobody can prove the link, and three readings compete:**

### 14.4 The Summer Flies: who fights whom (Tier 2+)
The lesser powers fight over what the high order no longer holds. Humans are terrain.
- **Kappa against river kami** for the fords: crossings demand a sacrifice to one side and offend the other.
- **Tengu against the yamabushi** for the mountain roads: passes close; the tengu take apprentices by force.
- **Kitsune split:** the zenko (Inari's) keep the rice stores; the yako sell protection and lie about it.
- **Oni clans** (Shuten-dōji's lineage and the Ōeyama remnants) come down from the mountains and take towns as fiefs; at Tier 3 they field companies. The Akaki Valleys' Red Gate (canon) is the front line in Tenmei.
- **Tanuki** run the black market in salt, rice and charms.
- **The Night Parade** splinters into rival processions that meet at crossroads and fight.
- **Tsukumogami** wake in the abandoned houses of the dead and refuse new owners.
- **Humans caught in it:** villages pay two patrons and are punished by both; bandits wear oni masks; shrines turn away the polluted refugees (PP, §3.3); onmyōji sell protection that sometimes works.

### 14.5 Electra's command threshold (P)
At **Tier 3**, when the court strips the saiō and its own remedy fails, the institutions that should run the crisis are visibly broken. From then on, **Electra may take charge of the mission** (the priests, Sōta, any locals bound to it) as its acting commander:
- the priests accept it on a reaction roll at +4 (the Calamity's evidence), or under her commission's authority if she chooses to reveal it;
- she can stand where men cannot (behind the saiō's blinds, in the divers' hut);
- the decision is hers to take and costs her the file's secrecy in part: commanding them means they learn she knew.
The threshold is a permission, not an obligation. Chad may move it to a different trigger.

## 15. Teruko, the saiō: the mirror as a woman (P; character approved in chat 7 Oct, no name check needed)

### 15.1 The premise
The Yata mirror, the Sun's spirit-seat, has not been looked on in centuries; it sits wrapped in its box at the Sun's Inner Shrine (G: at Watarai, Kozakura's Ise). **The bronze in the box is only bronze now.** Generations ago the mirror's spirit moved into the princess serving it, the way a kami settles into a vessel (*yorishiro*), and it has passed from saiō to saiō since.
- **The regalia are untouched.** The box is in the shrine, the emperor holds his three treasures, and the dynastic stop condition holds.
- **The precedent is the founding legend.** Princess Yamatohime carried the mirror across the provinces until the Sun chose Ise; her title was ***mitsueshiro*, "the august staff-substitute,"** the staff the god walks with. That is what Teruko has become.
- **Almost nobody knows.** Fewer than ten people: the high priest at Watarai, two court diviners, the emperor, and Teruko.

### 15.2 Who she is
**Princess Teruko** (照子, "shining child"), the emperor's half-sister. 34; saiō for 19 years. Small and straight-backed, with the stillness of a woman who has been watched since girlhood. Long black hair worn loose to the waist in the old court way, and on one side of it the **boxwood farewell comb** the emperor set there at her departure. White inner robes under layered robes of pale gold and vermilion; a sheer white veil hanging from a wide-brimmed hat, which she wears whenever she is outside her own rooms. Her eyes, when they can be seen, are dark and do not blink often enough. She writes poetry that the capital still copies, keeps a moss garden, and corresponds with a dozen women at court. She cannot lie and knows how rare that makes her.

### 15.3 How the tradition treats her (S, adapted)
- **Chosen by divination** (a cracked tortoise shell) at each reign's start from the unmarried princesses; neither she nor her family chooses.
- **Purified in stages:** a year in a hall inside the palace, a year at the **Nonomiya** (the brushwood-fenced palace in the fields), then the procession to Ise.
- **The farewell comb.** The emperor sets it in her hair and says one line: *"Do not turn toward the capital."* She may not look back.
- **Her own court.** She lives not at the shrine but at the **Saikū**, a walled town of about 500 officials, guards and ladies with its own government office. She enters the Inner Shrine three times a year (the rites of the 6th, 9th and 12th months).
- **Taboo words** inside the Saikū: death is "recovering," blood "sweat," weeping "dripping brine," illness "resting," a grave "a clod of earth," monks "long-hairs," sutras "dyed paper." **Saying "blood" in her hearing pollutes the compound** (+1 PP to the speaker, §3.3).
- **Nobody looks at her directly.** Men speak through bamboo blinds or a lady-in-waiting; being in a room with her unscreened is a scandal; **touching her is an offense against the Sun and the throne at once.**
- **Chastity is the institution.** The *Tales of Ise* (ep. 69) night between a courtier and the Ise saiō is the most famous scandal in the literature.
- **Release:** on the emperor's death or abdication, a close relative's death (mourning pollution), or her own impurity. On an emperor's death she returns by a different road, quietly. **For Teruko, release does not move the mirror; only her death does.**

### 15.4 What the court does to her in the Calamity (tied to §14.2)
1. **Stage 1, blamed (Tier 2).** The Bureau of Divination reads pollution and looks first at the saiō. Investigators at the Saikū; her ladies dismissed; her household purged around her.
2. **Stage 2, stripped (Tier 3).** The court releases her and divines a new saiō: a frightened 15-year-old princess (G, unnamed). Purification proceeds, the Calamity worsens, and nobody can say why. **Two saiō: one empty, one carrying the mirror and formally nobody.** By custom she must go back to the capital under escort.
3. **Stage 3, the cave faction (Tier 4).** Someone reads the myth correctly: if the Sun is in the cave, you hang the mirror at the door. A faction (onmyōji, Sun-shrine hardliners) moves to march Teruko to an Iwato, whether she survives the rite or not.

### 15.5 Build (both systems)
**3.5e (recommended): Aristocrat 1 / Cleric of Amaterasu 11 / Divine Oracle 4, Living Shintai template (+1). CR 16.** Domains Sun and Knowledge. Divine Oracle gives prophecy and an uncanny danger sense, so she is hard to ambush. Aristocrat 1 is the court (Diplomacy, Sense Motive, etiquette).
- Alternatives: **B** Aristocrat 1 / Shugenja (fire) 11 / Divine Oracle 4 (more native; Oracle prerequisites to verify against Shugenja); **C** Aristocrat 8 / Cleric 4 + template, ~CR 10 (pure court; the template carries her).
- **Why CR 16:** above the Hand (13–14), below Arik (18–20). The Hand cannot brush her aside, and Arik could end her with one bad decision. **She is killable by any of the Hand, and nobody should want to be the one who does it.**

**Living Shintai template (+1 CR):**
- **Unclouded:** with her eyes unveiled, everything within 30 ft is seen as it truly is: illusions, disguises, polymorphs, assumed identities and possessors fail in her sight (as *true seeing*), and she sees **the self a creature carries**.
- **The Face** (3/day, 30 ft): the target sees itself truly. Will DC 10 + ½ HD + Cha (≈22 at Cha 18). Failure: dazed 1 round, must answer one question about itself truthfully, and anything maintaining a false self (disguise, persona, possession, **implanted programming**) is suppressed for 1 minute.
- **Drawing Out** (1/day, 120 ft): the cave myth. One hidden or withdrawn being (invisible, ethereal, hiding, a god sulking in a vessel) must step into view on a failed Will save.
- **The Sun Looks Through Her:** Amaterasu can watch through her eyes at will; she always knows when.
- **Weaknesses:** she cannot lie (both systems); veiled, the template's powers are blind; she is subject to her own Face when she sees her reflection (the Saikū has no polished surfaces).

**GURPS (sketch):** ST 9, DX 11, IQ 14, HT 11; Will 18, Per 16. *Advantages:* Status 6, Clerical Investment, Power Investiture 5 (Amaterasu), Empathy, Danger Sense, See Invisible, Detect (Illusion/Shapeshift/Possession); the Face as Affliction 1 (Will-based; Daze + Truthfulness, 1 minute; Mental; Vision-based); Drawing Out as the same with a Reveal enhancement. *Disadvantages:* Truthfulness (cannot lie), Vow (chastity; never leave service without dispensation), Duty (Ise, 15-), Sense of Duty (the dynasty), Secret (the mirror). Point total set against the Hand's sheets at build (SR).

### 15.6 Teruko and Myth Pressure
- **She is the Wrecked Hall's maiden.** Arik's Susanoo clock's next beat is the Wrecked Hall (§2.5), where a weaving-maiden of the Sun died of the storm god's outrage. **Arik, or anyone acting the Susanoo role, within sight of the saiō in a hall of the Sun rhymes on Role and Place: two axes, a clock.** The myth wants her dead by the storm's hand.
- **She is also the cure.** The mirror is the only thing that has ever drawn the Sun out of hiding (§14.2, the rite at the cave).
- **The bead.** The Tooth-Marked Jewel is one of **the Sun's own jewels**, the one the storm god bit in the oath-contest. Unclouded sees it for what it is. Teruko knows the bead on sight, and the Sun, looking through her, knows it too.

### 14.7 The tiers in full (P; to choose the starting tier)
What a traveller sees, what the mission pays, and what the Pulse usually gives at each tier.
